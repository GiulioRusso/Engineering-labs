Decorator

What you are looking at

Left: the real C++ program, the line being executed highlighted. Right: the objects alive at this step (name : class, then their fields), grey arrows for the references they hold, the orange arrow for the call or return happening now. Underneath, what the program has printed. Press play, or step with the play bar.

Problem. A coffee can have milk, sugar, extra milk, syrup… in any combination. A subclass for every combination (EspressoWithMilkAndSugar) explodes in number and cannot be chosen at run time.

Solution. Each extra is a wrapper that both is a Coffee and has a Coffee inside. Its cost() asks the inner coffee and adds its own price. Wrappers can be stacked in any order and number, and the client only ever holds the outermost one.

  1. Decorators implement the same interface as the object they wrap.
  2. Each forwards the call inwards…
  3. …and adds its part on the way back out.

Where you meet it

Stream classes (a buffered, compressed, encrypted stream is three wrappers around a file), middleware in web servers, Python’s @decorator functions, adding logging or caching around a service.

Common mistakes

Depending on the concrete type of the object you hold (it is now a wrapper), and building chains so deep they are hard to debug.
