A loop can sit inside another. For every round of the outer loop, the inner loop runs all its rounds from the start. Here 3 rows × 3 columns make the inner body run 9 times; in general the work is the product of the two counts, which is why nested loops over large data get slow quickly.

Variables declared inside the outer loop, like line, are fresh for every row. Nested loops are how programs walk through grids, tables, images and every pair of items.

Common mistake

Reusing the same variable name, say i, for both loops: the inner loop overwrites the outer counter and the program skips rows or never ends.
