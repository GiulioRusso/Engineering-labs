Signals and their spectra

Any signal can be seen in time (top) or in frequency (bottom): which sines, of which amplitude, add up to it. A pure sine is a single line; sharp edges need many high harmonics.

$$x(t) = \sum_k A_k \sin(2\pi f_k t + \varphi_k)$$

Square wave

Only odd harmonics, with amplitudes \(\frac{4}{\pi n}\): \(f, 3f, 5f, \dots\). The jumps are what needs the high harmonics.

Sawtooth

Every harmonic is present, falling as \(1/n\).

Narrow pulses

Short in time, wide in frequency: the spectrum follows a sinc envelope whose first zero is at \(1/\tau\). Time width × bandwidth ≈ 1.

A chirp

The frequency rises linearly from 1 to 30 Hz over the record: the spectrum is a flat band, not a line. Radar uses chirps to get both energy and resolution.

White noise

Random samples, every frequency present with the same average power: a flat (but ragged) spectrum.

A sine in noise

In time the sine is hard to see; in frequency it stands out as one line above the noise floor. That is why receivers look at spectra.

Fourier series

A periodic signal is a sum of sines at multiples of its frequency. Each term is a circle turning \(n\) times per period with radius \(|b_n|\): stack the circles (epicycles) and the tip of the last one traces the signal.

$$x(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{N} b_n \sin(2\pi n f t), \qquad b_n = 2f\int_0^{1/f} x(t)\sin(2\pi n f t)\,dt$$

One harmonic

The best single sine for a square wave has amplitude \(4/\pi \approx 1.27\): bigger than the square itself.

Three harmonics

\(\frac4\pi\left(\sin x + \frac{\sin 3x}{3} + \frac{\sin 5x}{5}\right)\): the corners start to form.

The Gibbs phenomenon

With 50 harmonics the sum is flat almost everywhere, but near each jump it still overshoots by about 9 % of the jump. More terms make the overshoot narrower, never smaller.

Sawtooth

All harmonics with alternating signs, \(\frac{2}{\pi n}\). One jump per period, one Gibbs overshoot.

Triangle

No jumps, only corners: coefficients fall as \(1/n^2\), so three harmonics already look right.

Sampling and aliasing

Sampling keeps only the values at \(t = n/f_s\). If \(f_s > 2f\) (the Nyquist rate) the samples determine the signal. Otherwise a lower frequency fits the same samples exactly: an alias, folded into \([0, f_s/2]\).

$$f_s > 2 f_{\max}, \qquad f_\text{alias} = |f - k f_s| \text{ (nearest)}$$

2.5 samples per period

Not pretty, but enough: only \(f\) fits these samples below \(f_s/2\).

Exactly 2f

Two samples per period: depending on the phase they can all be zero. The Nyquist condition is a strict inequality.

An alias

7 Hz sampled at 10 Hz gives the same samples as 3 Hz (\(10 - 7\)). After sampling, nothing can tell them apart: filter before you sample.

The wagon wheel

9.5 Hz at 10 Hz looks like 0.5 Hz: the wheel in a film seems to turn slowly, or backwards.

The DFT

The discrete Fourier transform turns \(N\) samples into \(N\) frequency bins, one cycle per record apart. The FFT computes the same numbers in \(N\log N\) steps instead of \(N^2\).

$$X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}, \qquad \Delta f = \frac{f_s}{N}$$

Two tones

Each sine lands on its own bin with its amplitude: 1 at bin 8, 0.5 at bin 13.

Leakage

8.5 cycles do not fit the record: the cut ends make the tone spread over many bins, and its peak drops (scalloping).

Resolution

With 16 samples the bins are too coarse: tones 0.6 bins apart merge into one peak.

A longer record

256 samples of the same signal give bins 16 times finer: the two tones separate. Resolution needs observation time, \(\Delta f \approx 1/T\).

Windows

Multiplying the record by a smooth window tapers its ends, so the cut does not throw energy into far bins. The price: a wider main peak. In dB the difference is dramatic.

$$X_w[k] = \sum_n w[n]\,x[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$$

Hann

Sidelobes fall from about −13 dB (rectangular) to −31 dB and keep falling fast.

Blackman

Sidelobes below −58 dB: the −40 dB tone at bin 16, buried in leakage before, is now visible. Window choice sets the dynamic range of a spectrum analyser.

On a bin

A tone with a whole number of cycles has no leakage with any window: the rectangular window is then the sharpest.
