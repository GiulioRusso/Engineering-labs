The line

Two points fix a line. The slope \(m\) is rise over run, the same everywhere; \(q\) is where it crosses the \(y\) axis. Drag the two handles.

$$y = m x + q, \qquad m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

Slope zero

Same height at both points: \(m = 0\), the line is horizontal, \(y = q\). It never crosses the \(x\) axis unless \(q = 0\).

Through the origin

\(q = 0\): \(y = m x\). Doubling \(x\) doubles \(y\): direct proportion.

Negative slope

\(m < 0\): the line falls to the right. The root is at \(x = -q/m\).

Steep

One step right, four steps up: \(m = 4\). The bigger \(|m|\), the steeper.

The parabola

The vertex form shows the turning point \((h, k)\); \(a\) sets the opening. Drag the vertex. The discriminant \(\Delta\) counts the real roots.

$$y = a(x-h)^2 + k = a x^2 + b x + c, \qquad x_{1,2} = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt\Delta}{2a}, \quad \Delta = b^2 - 4ac$$

Two roots

The vertex is below the axis and the parabola opens up, so it crosses twice: \(\Delta > 0\). Here \(y = x^2 - 2\), roots \(\pm\sqrt 2\).

One root

The vertex sits on the axis: \(\Delta = 0\), a double root at \(x = h\). Here \(y = (x-1)^2\).

No roots

Vertex above the axis, opening up: the curve never reaches \(y = 0\). \(\Delta < 0\): the roots are complex.

Opens down

\(a < 0\) flips the parabola: the vertex is now a maximum.

Wider

Small \(|a|\): \(y\) grows slowly away from the vertex, so the parabola is wide. Large \(|a|\) makes it narrow.

The exponential

Every step of 1 in \(x\) multiplies \(y\) by the same factor \(b\). It starts at \(A\) for \(x = 0\) and never reaches zero: the \(x\) axis is an asymptote.

$$y = A\,b^x, \qquad y(x + 1) = b\,y(x)$$

Decay

\(0 < b < 1\): each step halves \(y\) (here \(b = \tfrac12\)). Same curve as \(2^{-x}\): the growth curve mirrored left to right.

Base e

\(e \approx 2.718\) is the base whose curve has slope equal to its height at every point: \((e^x)' = e^x\).

Starting value

\(A\) is the value at \(x = 0\). Changing it stretches the curve vertically; the doubling stays the same.

The inverse

Swap \(x\) and \(y\): the mirror across \(y = x\) is the logarithm. It answers “which power gives \(y\)?”.

$$y = A b^x \iff x = \log_b \frac{y}{A}$$

The logarithm

\(\log_b x\) is the power you raise \(b\) to in order to get \(x\). It exists only for \(x > 0\), crosses the axis at \(x = 1\) and reaches 1 at \(x = b\): drag that handle.

$$y = \log_b x \iff b^y = x, \qquad \log_b(xy) = \log_b x + \log_b y$$

Natural logarithm

\(\ln x = \log_e x\), the inverse of \(e^x\). Its slope at \(x\) is \(1/x\).

Base below 1

With \(b < 1\) the logarithm falls: bigger \(x\) needs a more negative power of \(b\).

Mirror of the exponential

The dashed curve is \(2^x\), the mirror image across \(y = x\). Every point \((a, b)\) of one is \((b, a)\) on the other.
