The line

What you are looking at

A straight line through two points you can drag. The readout gives its slope m, where it crosses the y axis (q) and where it crosses the x axis (the root).

A function is a machine: put in x, get out y. For a line the machine is the simplest possible: multiply by m, add q. Because it multiplies by the same number everywhere, every step of 1 to the right changes y by exactly m — that is why the graph is straight.

$$y = m x + q, \qquad m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$$
  1. Slope from two points: subtract the heights, subtract the x positions, divide. The order does not matter, as long as it is the same on top and bottom.
  2. Intercept: put one point into y = mx + q and solve for q.
  3. Root: set y = 0: x = −q/m (only if m ≠ 0).

Worked example

Points (−2, −1) and (2, 2): m = (2 − (−1)) / (2 − (−2)) = 3/4. Then −1 = (3/4)(−2) + q gives q = 0.5. Root: x = −0.5/0.75 = −0.67. Check with the other point: (3/4)·2 + 0.5 = 2 ✓.

Common mistakes

Mixing the order: (y₂ − y₁)/(x₁ − x₂) gives the wrong sign. Thinking a steep-looking line has a big slope: it depends on the axis scales — compute it. A vertical line is not a function (one x, many y): its slope is undefined.

Try it

Drag one point and watch m, q and the root update. Make the two heights equal: the slope becomes 0.

Slope zero

Same height at both points: \(m = 0\), the line is horizontal, \(y = q\). It never crosses the \(x\) axis unless \(q = 0\).

Through the origin

\(q = 0\): \(y = m x\). Doubling \(x\) doubles \(y\): direct proportion.

Negative slope

\(m < 0\): the line falls to the right. The root is at \(x = -q/m\).

Steep

One step right, four steps up: \(m = 4\). The bigger \(|m|\), the steeper.

The parabola

What you are looking at

A parabola with its vertex (draggable) and its roots marked where it crosses the x axis. The readout shows both forms of the equation and the discriminant Δ.

Squaring makes every number positive and grows faster and faster: that is the U shape. The vertex form tells you where the U sits — its turning point (h, k) — and how wide it opens (a). The expanded form ax² + bx + c is the same curve written differently.

$$y = a(x-h)^2 + k = ax^2 + bx + c, \qquad h = -\frac{b}{2a}, \qquad x_{1,2} = \frac{-b \pm\sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}, \quad \Delta = b^2 - 4ac$$
  1. Where does it turn? At x = h; its height there is k.
  2. Which way? a > 0 opens up (k is a minimum); a < 0 opens down (k is a maximum).
  3. Does it cross the axis? Only if the vertex and the opening point in opposite directions — that is exactly the sign of Δ.

Worked example

y = x² − 4x + 3: a = 1, b = −4, c = 3. Vertex h = 4/2 = 2, k = 4 − 8 + 3 = −1. Δ = 16 − 12 = 4 > 0, roots (4 ± 2)/2 = 1 and 3. They sit symmetrically around h = 2 — the parabola is a mirror image of itself about its vertex.

Common mistakes

Forgetting the ± (two roots, not one). Writing y = (x − 2)² and reading the vertex as x = −2: in vertex form the sign flips. Dividing by 2 only the √Δ: the whole numerator is divided by 2a.

Try it

Drag the vertex slowly up through the x axis: the two roots move together, merge (Δ = 0) and disappear (Δ < 0).

Two roots

The vertex is below the axis and the parabola opens up, so it crosses twice: \(\Delta > 0\). Here \(y = x^2 - 2\), roots \(\pm\sqrt 2\).

One root

The vertex sits on the axis: \(\Delta = 0\), a double root at \(x = h\). Here \(y = (x-1)^2\).

No roots

Vertex above the axis, opening up: the curve never reaches \(y = 0\). \(\Delta < 0\): the roots are complex.

Opens down

\(a < 0\) flips the parabola: the vertex is now a maximum.

Wider

Small \(|a|\): \(y\) grows slowly away from the vertex, so the parabola is wide. Large \(|a|\) makes it narrow.

The exponential

What you are looking at

The curve y = A·bˣ, with dots at whole-number x showing that each step right multiplies the height by b.

A line adds the same amount every step; an exponential multiplies by the same factor every step. That is why it starts slowly and then explodes (b > 1), or collapses toward zero without ever reaching it (b < 1). Money with interest, bacteria, radioactive decay and the charge on a capacitor all behave this way.

$$y = A\,b^{x}, \qquad \frac{y(x+1)}{y(x)} = b, \qquad b^{x}b^{y} = b^{x+y}$$
  1. At x = 0, b⁰ = 1, so y = A: A is the starting value.
  2. Each +1 in x multiplies y by b; each −1 divides by b.
  3. The doubling time (b > 1) is the x step that doubles y: log_b 2.

Worked example

A population of 100 that grows 50 % a year: y = 100·1.5ˣ. After 4 years 100·5.06 = 506. Doubling time log₁.₅ 2 = 1.71 years. A linear growth of +50 a year would give only 300.

Common mistakes

Confusing 2ˣ with x²: the first doubles at every step, the second grows by adding. Thinking the curve hits zero for b < 1: it only gets closer and closer.

Try it

Count the dots: each is b times the previous one. Change the base to 0.5 and the same dots halve.

Decay

\(0 < b < 1\): each step halves \(y\) (here \(b = \tfrac12\)). Same curve as \(2^{-x}\): the growth curve mirrored left to right.

Base e

\(e \approx 2.718\) is the base whose curve has slope equal to its height at every point: \((e^x)' = e^x\).

Starting value

\(A\) is the value at \(x = 0\). Changing it stretches the curve vertically; the doubling stays the same.

The inverse

Swap \(x\) and \(y\): the mirror across \(y = x\) is the logarithm. It answers “which power gives \(y\)?”.

$$y = A b^x \iff x = \log_b \frac{y}{A}$$

The logarithm

What you are looking at

y = log_b x, with the handle at (b, 1) and the point (1, 0) marked; the dashed curve is the exponential it undoes.

The logarithm answers a question: to what power must I raise b to get x? It is the exponential run backwards, which is why its graph is the exponential's mirror image across the line y = x. It turns multiplication into addition, so it squeezes huge ranges into small ones — decibels, pH and the Richter scale are all logarithms.

$$y = \log_b x \iff b^{y} = x, \qquad \log_b(xy) = \log_b x + \log_b y, \qquad \log_b x^n = n\log_b x$$
  1. log_b 1 = 0 for every base (b⁰ = 1): every log curve passes through (1, 0).
  2. log_b b = 1: that is the handle.
  3. x must be positive: no power of a positive base gives zero or a negative number.

Worked example

log₂ 8 = 3 because 2³ = 8. log₂ 1000 ≈ 9.97 (2¹⁰ = 1024). In decibels: a factor 1000 in power is 10·log₁₀ 1000 = 30 dB; a factor 2 is 3 dB.

Common mistakes

Writing log(x + y) = log x + log y — false; the rule is for products. Taking the log of a negative number. Mixing bases: log, ln and log₂ differ by constant factors (ln x = 2.303·log₁₀ x).

Try it

Drag the base handle: the curve always passes through (1, 0) and through (b, 1).

Natural logarithm

\(\ln x = \log_e x\), the inverse of \(e^x\). Its slope at \(x\) is \(1/x\).

Base below 1

With \(b < 1\) the logarithm falls: bigger \(x\) needs a more negative power of \(b\).

Mirror of the exponential

The dashed curve is \(2^x\), the mirror image across \(y = x\). Every point \((a, b)\) of one is \((b, a)\) on the other.
