The unit circle

A point goes round a circle of radius 1. Its height is the sine of the angle, its horizontal position the cosine. Unroll the angle along a third axis and the point traces a helix whose shadows are the familiar waves.

Sine and cosine are coordinates

$$P(\theta) = (\cos\theta,\ \sin\theta), \qquad \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$$

The identity is just Pythagoras on the right triangle under the point.

The helix and its shadows

Move the angle along the \(x\) axis and the point draws a helix. Seen from the side it is the sine wave; seen from below, the cosine wave: the same motion, a quarter turn apart.

$$\cos\theta = \sin\!\left(\theta + \tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$$

The tangent

Extend the radius until it meets the vertical line \(x = 1\): the height there is \(\tan\theta\). It blows up at \(90°\), where the radius is parallel to that line.

$$\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$$

Radians

An angle in radians is the length of arc it cuts on the unit circle: a full turn is \(2\pi\).

$$\theta_{\text{rad}} = \theta_{\text{deg}}\cdot\frac{\pi}{180}$$
