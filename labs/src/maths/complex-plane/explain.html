Complex numbers

A complex number \(z = a + bi\) is a point in the plane: real part across, imaginary part up. Every operation is a piece of geometry. Drag \(z\) and \(w\).

Adding is joining arrows

$$z + w = (a + c) + (b + d)\,i$$

The sum is the diagonal of the parallelogram built on the two arrows.

Multiplying is rotating and stretching

In polar form a number is a length and an angle. Multiplying multiplies the lengths and adds the angles; multiplying by \(i\) is a quarter turn.

$$z = r e^{i\theta}, \qquad z\,w = r_z r_w\, e^{i(\theta_z + \theta_w)}$$

Powers spiral

$$z^n = r^n e^{i n\theta} \quad\text{(de Moivre)}$$

If \(|z| > 1\) the powers spiral out, if \(|z| < 1\) they spiral in, and on the unit circle they just turn.

Roots sit on a regular polygon

$$\sqrt[n]{z} = r^{1/n} e^{i(\theta + 2\pi k)/n}, \quad k = 0, \dots, n-1$$

Every non-zero number has exactly \(n\) different \(n\)-th roots, evenly spaced around a circle. They add up to zero.
