A complex number \(z = a + bi\) is a point in the plane: real part across, imaginary part up. In polar form it is a length and an angle. Drag \(z\) and \(w\).

$$z = a + bi = r\,e^{i\theta}, \qquad r = |z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}, \quad i^2 = -1$$

Adding is joining arrows

Add real parts and imaginary parts separately. The sum is the diagonal of the parallelogram on the two arrows.

$$z + w = (a + c) + (b + d)\,i$$

Two real numbers

On the real axis, complex addition is ordinary addition: \(2 + 1 = 3\).

Number plus conjugate

\(\bar z = a - bi\) is the mirror image across the real axis. The imaginary parts cancel: \(z + \bar z = 2a\).

Opposite numbers

\(-z\) points the other way; the parallelogram collapses and the sum is 0.

Multiplying: rotate and stretch

Lengths multiply, angles add.

$$z\,w = r_z r_w\, e^{i(\theta_z + \theta_w)}$$

Times a real number

\(w = 2\) has angle 0: the product is \(z\) stretched twice as long, same direction.

Times i

\(i\) has length 1 and angle 90°: multiplying by it is a quarter turn, no stretch. \((2 + i)\,i = -1 + 2i\).

i times i

Two quarter turns make a half turn: \(i^2 = -1\). This is why \(i\) is needed: no real number squares to \(-1\).

Number times conjugate

The angles \(\theta\) and \(-\theta\) cancel: the product is real and equals \(|z|^2\). Here \((1 + i)(1 - i) = 2\).

Dividing: rotate back and shrink

Lengths divide, angles subtract.

$$\frac{z}{w} = \frac{r_z}{r_w}\, e^{i(\theta_z - \theta_w)} = \frac{z\,\bar w}{|w|^2}$$

A number over itself

Same length, same angle: the quotient is 1.

Divide by i

Undo a quarter turn: \(z / i = -i\,z\), a turn of −90°.

Powers spiral

Each power multiplies by \(z\) again: the angle steps by \(\theta\), the length by \(r\) (de Moivre).

$$z^n = r^n e^{i n\theta}$$

Spiralling out

\(|z| > 1\): every power is longer than the last.

Spiralling in

\(|z| < 1\): the powers shrink towards 0.

Powers of i

\(i, -1, -i, 1\): four quarter turns bring you back. The powers repeat every 4.

Roots sit on a regular polygon

Every non-zero number has exactly \(n\) different \(n\)-th roots, evenly spaced around a circle of radius \(r^{1/n}\). They add up to zero.

$$\sqrt[n]{z} = r^{1/n} e^{i(\theta + 2\pi k)/n}, \quad k = 0, \dots, n-1$$

Square roots of 1

\(1\) and \(-1\): both square to 1, half a turn apart.

Sixth roots of unity

Six points 60° apart: a regular hexagon.

Square roots of i

\(i\) is at 90°, so its roots are at 45° and 225°: \(\pm\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}(1 + i)\).

Cube roots of −1

\(-1\) itself is one of them; the other two are \(\tfrac12 \pm \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} i\).
