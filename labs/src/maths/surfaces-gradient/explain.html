Surfaces and the gradient

A function of two variables is a landscape: height \(z = f(x, y)\) over the floor. Drag the point to walk on it.

The gradient points uphill

Its components are the slopes in the \(x\) and \(y\) directions. Together they point the way the ground rises fastest, and their length is that steepest slope.

$$\nabla f = \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x},\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)$$

…and it is perpendicular to the level curves

Walking along a contour line keeps the height constant, so the slope along it is zero: the gradient has no component along the contour.

$$\nabla f \cdot \mathbf t = 0 \quad\text{for any } \mathbf t \text{ tangent to } f = \text{const}$$

The tangent plane

Close to the point the surface is almost flat. The best flat approximation uses the value and the gradient there. Its error grows like the square of the distance.

$$z \approx f(x_0, y_0) + \nabla f(x_0, y_0)\cdot(x - x_0,\ y - y_0)$$
