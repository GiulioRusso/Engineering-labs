A \(3\times3\) matrix moves every point of space. Its columns are where the three unit vectors land (red, green, blue arrows), and that is enough to know where everything goes. Drag the arrow tips. The cube's volume is \(\det A\); yellow lines are eigenvectors.

$$A\mathbf x = x_1\mathbf c_1 + x_2\mathbf c_2 + x_3\mathbf c_3, \qquad \det A = \mathbf c_1\cdot(\mathbf c_2\times\mathbf c_3), \qquad A\mathbf v = \lambda\mathbf v$$

Stretch

A diagonal matrix scales each axis by its own factor. The axes are the eigenvectors, the factors the eigenvalues, the volume their product.

Identity

\(I\) leaves every point where it is: \(\det I = 1\), every vector is an eigenvector with \(\lambda = 1\).

Uniform scale

Every direction is stretched by 1.5, so every vector is an eigenvector. The volume grows by \(1.5^3 = 3.375\).

Squash

Only \(z\) shrinks, to 0.3: the volume is 0.3.

Rotation

Lengths and angles are kept, so the volume stays 1. Only the axis keeps its direction: it is the one real eigenvector (\(\lambda = 1\)).

$$R_z(\varphi) = \begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi & -\sin\varphi & 0\\ \sin\varphi & \cos\varphi & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$$

A quarter turn

\(x\) goes to \(y\), \(y\) to \(-x\). Two of the eigenvalues are complex, \(\pm i\).

A half turn

Now every vector in the \(xy\) plane is flipped: eigenvalue \(-1\), twice. Still \(\det = 1\): turning is not mirroring.

Shear

Layers slide over each other like a deck of cards. The volume does not change: \(\det = 1\).

Gentle shear

The top of the cube moves 0.4 along \(x\). The \(x\) axis is the only eigenvector.

Along z

Higher layers slide diagonally; the floor stays fixed.

Reflection

A mirror flips one direction: \(\det = -1\), space turned inside out (a right hand becomes a left hand).

Mirror in the floor

\(z \to -z\): the cube hangs below the floor.

Through the origin

\(-I\) flips all three axes: \(\det = (-1)^3 = -1\).

Projection

Space is flattened onto a plane: the cube has no volume, \(\det = 0\). Information is lost, so there is no inverse.

Straight down

Every point drops to \(z = 0\): the shadow at noon.

Onto a line

Only one column is left: the whole space lands on the \(x\) axis. The rank (dimension of the image) is 1.

Any matrix

This one is symmetric, so its three eigenvectors are real and at right angles: in that frame it is just a stretch.

The inverse

\(A^{-1}\) undoes \(A\): its determinant is \(1/\det A\) and it has the same eigenvectors with eigenvalues \(1/\lambda\).
