A differential equation gives the slope at every point: the short grey segments. A solution is a curve that follows them everywhere; drag the starting point to pick one. Euler's method (violet) walks in straight steps of length \(h\) along the slope at the start of each step.

$$y' = f(x, y), \qquad y_{n+1} = y_n + h\,f(x_n, y_n), \qquad \text{error} \propto h$$

Exponential growth

The slope is proportional to the value: the more there is, the faster it grows (money at interest, bacteria, radioactive decay for \(k < 0\)).

$$y' = k y \;\Rightarrow\; y = y_0\,e^{k(x - x_0)}$$

Decay

\(k < 0\): every solution falls towards 0, and halves every \(\ln 2/|k|\).

A smaller step

With \(h = 0.1\) Euler hugs the true curve. Its error is proportional to \(h\): five times smaller step, five times smaller error.

A big step

Each step uses the slope at its start, which is too small for a growing curve: Euler falls further and further below.

Numerical instability

For \(y' = -3y\) each Euler step multiplies by \(1 - 3h\). With \(h = 0.8\) that is \(-1.4\): the numbers flip sign and grow, although the true solution decays. Euler is stable only for \(h < 2/3\) here.

Logistic growth

Growth that slows as it approaches the capacity \(K\): an S-curve. \(y = 0\) and \(y = K\) are equilibria: flat slopes.

$$y' = r y\left(1 - \frac{y}{K}\right) \;\Rightarrow\; y = \frac{K y_0}{y_0 + (K - y_0)e^{-r(x - x_0)}}$$

Above the capacity

Too many: the slope is negative and the population falls back to \(K\). \(K\) is a stable equilibrium.

At equilibrium

Starting exactly at \(K\) the slope is 0 forever: the solution is a horizontal line.

Faster growth

A larger \(r\) makes the S steeper; the end value \(K\) does not change.

A driven system

The solution forgets its start: the difference from the steady curve decays as \(e^{-x}\), and every solution ends up following the drive.

$$y' = -y + \cos x \;\Rightarrow\; y = \tfrac12(\cos x + \sin x) + C e^{-x}$$

From below

A different start, the same steady oscillation after a few units of \(x\): the transient \(Ce^{-x}\) dies out.

On the steady curve

Starting on it, \(C = 0\): there is no transient at all.

The phase plane

A second-order equation becomes two first-order ones: position \(x\) across, velocity \(x'\) up. Each point is a state, the field shows where it goes next. Undamped oscillations are circles: the energy \(x^2 + x'^2\) is constant.

$$x'' = -x - 2\gamma x' \iff \begin{cases} x' = v \\ v' = -x - 2\gamma v \end{cases}$$

Damped

Friction drains energy: the circle becomes a spiral into the rest point at the origin.

Euler gains energy

Each Euler step on a circle moves along the tangent, slightly outwards: the energy grows by \(1 + h^2\) per step and the orbit spirals out. Better methods (like RK4, used for the blue curve) avoid this.

Overdamped

With \(\gamma > 1\) the state slides into the origin without ever turning around it.
