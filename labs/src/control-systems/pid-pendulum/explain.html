PID control

A motor at the pivot pushes the rod back towards upright. The controller looks at the error (the tilt) three ways: how big it is now (P), how much it has added up (I), and how fast it is changing (D).

$$\tau = -K_p\,\theta - K_i\!\int\!\theta\,dt - K_d\,\dot\theta, \qquad mL^2\ddot\theta = mgL\sin\theta + \tau + \tau_\text{dist}$$

P is a spring: it must beat gravity, \(K_p > mgL\). D is a damper: it calms the swinging but amplifies sensor noise. I removes any steady error, but can overshoot and wind up when the motor saturates.

Kp too small

With \(K_p = 3 < mgL = 4.9\) N·m/rad, gravity pulls harder than the controller pushes back: the rod falls, slowly at first.

P only

A pure spring: the rod rocks back and forth around upright, damped only by the small friction in the pivot. The linearised equation is \(mL^2\ddot\theta = -(K_p - mgL)\theta\).

A push

A short torque kick at 1 s. More \(K_d\) absorbs it with less swinging; too little and it rocks for longer.

Noise and the D term

The sensor jitters by a fraction of a degree. D reacts to the rate of change, and noise changes very fast: the motor torque (violet) shakes. Real controllers filter the derivative (here over 10 ms) and keep \(K_d\) moderate.

A load

From 3 s a constant load torque leans on the rod. PD can only push back if the rod is tilted: it settles where \(K_p\theta = mgL\sin\theta + \tau_\text{load}\), about 7.6° here.

Integral action

The integral keeps growing while any tilt remains, so the motor keeps adding torque until the tilt is exactly zero. The cost: a slower, slightly oscillatory recovery.

Integrator windup

The motor can only give 6 N·m. Starting at 30°, it saturates; meanwhile the integral keeps piling up. When the rod finally comes back, the huge stored integral pushes it far past upright, beyond what the weak motor can recover: it falls.

Anti-windup

Stop integrating while the motor is saturated: the integral stays small, and the rod comes back without the big overshoot.

About the model

Point mass on a massless rod, pivot friction \(b\), controller at 1 kHz with a 10 ms derivative filter, integration by semi-implicit Euler. Tests: falls for \(K_p < mgL\), holds above; PD load tilt satisfies the torque balance (10⁻³); integral removes it; windup falls, anti-windup recovers.
