Gas in a piston

Every dot is an atom of argon (each one standing for many real atoms). The pressure is not computed from a formula: it is measured from the momentum the atoms deliver when they bounce off the walls.

Pressure is momentum delivered

Each bounce reverses an atom's velocity across the wall and pushes it with \(2mv_\perp\). Adding up all the bounces per second per square metre gives the ideal gas law.

$$p = \frac{1}{3}\frac{N m \langle v^2\rangle}{V} = \frac{N k_B T}{V}, \qquad \tfrac32 k_B T = \langle \tfrac12 m v^2 \rangle$$

Squeezing heats the gas

An atom bouncing off a piston that moves inward comes back faster, like a ball off a swung bat. With no heat flowing through the walls the gas warms up as it is compressed, and cools as it expands.

$$p V^\gamma = \text{const}, \quad T V^{\gamma - 1} = \text{const}, \qquad \gamma = \tfrac53 \text{ (monatomic)}$$

Isothermal: heat leaves as fast as work comes in

With walls held at fixed temperature (a thermostat), compressing does not warm the gas: the work done flows out as heat.

$$pV = nRT = \text{const}, \qquad W = nRT\ln\frac{V_2}{V_1}$$

The speeds follow Maxwell–Boltzmann

$$f(v) = 4\pi\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_B T}\right)^{3/2} v^2 e^{-m v^2/2k_BT}$$
About the model

Point atoms, elastic walls; the lid is a heavy moving wall (\(v' = 2u - v\)). Atom–atom collisions are modelled as isotropic scattering at \(10^5\) per second, keeping each speed, so energy is shared between directions as in a real gas. Tests: measured pressure vs \(NkT/V\) within 3 % (statistical); slow adiabatic halving of the volume heats by ×1.61 vs the ideal ×1.587.
