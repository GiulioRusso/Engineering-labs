A charge in electric and magnetic fields

What you are looking at

A charged particle moving through a uniform magnetic field B (and optionally an electric field E), its trail, and the force and velocity arrows.

The electric force pushes along E and changes the particle's speed. The magnetic force is always at right angles to the velocity: it can only turn the particle, never speed it up. A force that always points sideways makes a circle — or, if the particle also moves along B, a helix.

$$\mathbf F = q(\mathbf E + \mathbf v\times\mathbf B), \qquad r = \frac{m v_\perp}{|q|B}, \qquad T = \frac{2\pi m}{|q|B}, \qquad \mathbf v_d = \frac{\mathbf E\times\mathbf B}{B^2}$$
  1. Radius grows with speed, but the period does not depend on speed: fast particles take bigger circles in the same time (this is how a cyclotron works).
  2. The velocity component along B is untouched: the circle is stretched into a helix.
  3. E across B makes the circle drift sideways at E/B — in the same direction for both signs of charge.

Worked example

A proton at 10 km/s in 1 mT: r = 1.67·10⁻²⁷·10⁴ / (1.6·10⁻¹⁹·10⁻³) = 0.104 m; T = 65.6 µs. With E = 1 V/m the drift is 1000 m/s.

Common mistakes

Thinking B does work on the charge: F ⊥ v, so no work, constant speed. Getting the turning direction wrong for negative charges: the force reverses.

Try it

Double the speed: the circle doubles, the period stays. Tilt the velocity: watch the helix pitch.

Circles

Velocity at right angles to a uniform \(\mathbf B\): the force always points to the same centre, so the particle circles at the cyclotron frequency, whatever its speed.

$$\omega_c = \frac{|q| B}{m}, \qquad r_L = \frac{m\,v_\perp}{|q| B}$$

Stronger field

Three times \(B\): a circle three times smaller, turned three times faster.

Faster particle

The radius grows with \(v\), but the period does not change: this is what makes a cyclotron work.

Opposite charge

\(q \to -q\) flips the force: the antiparticle circles the other way, same size.

An electron

1836 times lighter than a proton: it circles 1836 times faster, on a much smaller circle at the same speed.

Helices

The part of the velocity along \(\mathbf B\) feels no force, so the circle is stretched along \(\mathbf B\) into a helix. This is how charged particles spiral along the Earth's field lines into the aurora.

$$p = v_\parallel \cdot \frac{2\pi}{\omega_c}$$

Almost along B

A small angle: tiny circles, long pitch. The particle is guided by the field line.

The E × B drift

With \(\mathbf E\) across \(\mathbf B\), the particle speeds up on one half of its circle and slows on the other, so the loops do not close: it drifts at right angles to both fields.

$$\mathbf v_d = \frac{\cE{\mathbf E}\times\cB{\mathbf B}}{B^2}$$

Same drift for both

The antiparticle circles the opposite way but drifts in the same direction at the same speed: \(\mathbf v_d\) contains neither \(q\) nor \(m\).

Stronger E

The drift speed is \(E/B\): three times the field, three times faster.

E along B

Now the electric force pushes along the axis: the helix stretches as the particle speeds up. The kinetic energy rises, all from \(q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v\).

E against the motion

The particle slows along \(\mathbf B\), stops, and is pushed back: the helix closes up and reverses.

A helix

The velocity along \(\mathbf B\) feels no force; only the part across \(\mathbf B\) circles. Together: a helix with pitch \(v_\parallel \cdot 2\pi/\omega_c\).

E × B drift

Faster on one half of the circle, slower on the other: the loops do not close, and the particle drifts at \(E/B\), at right angles to both fields.

$$\mathbf v_d = \frac{\cE{\mathbf E}\times\cB{\mathbf B}}{B^2}$$

E along B

The electric force pushes along the axis: the helix stretches as the particle speeds up, gaining energy \(q\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf v\).

About the model

Boris pusher with 200 steps per gyration: half electric kick, exact magnetic rotation, half kick. In a pure magnetic field it conserves the speed to round-off (test: \(10^{-12}\)), and the drift matches \(E/B\) within 0.2 %.
