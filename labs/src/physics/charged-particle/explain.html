A charged particle in E and B

The particle feels the Lorentz force. The electric part pushes along \(\mathbf E\); the magnetic part pushes sideways, at right angles to both the velocity and \(\mathbf B\).

The Lorentz force

$$\cF{\mathbf F} = q\left(\cE{\mathbf E} + \cv{\mathbf v}\times\cB{\mathbf B}\right)$$

Because the magnetic force is always perpendicular to the velocity, it does no work: it bends the path but never changes the speed.

Circles and helices

In a uniform \(\mathbf B\) the particle circles at the cyclotron frequency, which does not depend on its speed. Faster particles just make bigger circles. Any velocity along \(\mathbf B\) is untouched, so the circle stretches into a helix.

$$\omega_c = \frac{|q| B}{m}, \qquad r_L = \frac{m\,v_\perp}{|q| B}$$

The E × B drift

Add an electric field across \(\mathbf B\). The particle speeds up on one half of its circle and slows down on the other, so the loops do not close: it drifts sideways, at right angles to \(\mathbf E\). The drift has the same speed and direction for any charge or mass.

$$\mathbf v_d = \frac{\cE{\mathbf E}\times\cB{\mathbf B}}{B^2}$$
About the model

Boris pusher with 200 steps per gyration: half electric kick, exact magnetic rotation, half kick. In a pure magnetic field it conserves the speed to round-off (test: \(10^{-12}\)), and the drift matches \(E/B\) within 0.2 %.
