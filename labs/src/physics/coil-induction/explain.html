One loop of current

Every short piece of wire adds a little field, perpendicular to both the wire and the line to the point (Biot–Savart). Summed around a ring, the field threads through the middle and curls back around the wire outside. Far away the ring is a magnetic dipole: the field fades as \(1/r^3\).

$$\cB{B_z}(z) = \frac{\mu_0 \cI{I} R^2}{2\,(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}, \qquad \cB{B}(0) = \frac{\mu_0 \cI{I}}{2R}$$

Double the current

The field is proportional to \(I\): every value on the axis plot doubles. The shape of the lines does not change.

A wider loop

At the centre \(B = \mu_0 I/2R\): a loop 1.6 times wider gives a field 1.6 times weaker there, but it reaches further along the axis.

Many loops: the solenoid

Side by side, the rings' fields add up inside and nearly cancel outside. In a long coil the field inside is uniform and depends only on the turns per metre \(n = N/L\), not on the radius. At an end it is half the central value.

$$\cB{B} \approx \mu_0\, n\, \cI{I}$$

A solenoid

150 loops side by side over 60 cm. Inside, their fields add up to a nearly uniform \(B = \mu_0 n I\); outside they almost cancel. The model curve sits on the dashed formula.

More turns per metre

200 turns on 40 cm: \(n = 500\)/m instead of 250/m, so twice the field inside.

Wider, same n

Doubling the radius leaves the inside field unchanged: \(\mu_0 n I\) has no \(R\) in it (as long as the coil stays long compared to its width).

Short and wide

When \(L\) is smaller than \(R\) the field is no longer uniform: it peaks in the middle and the lines spread out widely (fringing). The coil behaves more like a single loop.

Flux through a loop

Flux counts how much field passes through the surface bounded by a loop. It is proportional to the source current; the constant \(M\) (mutual inductance) depends only on the shapes and positions.

$$\Phi = \int_S \cB{\mathbf B}\cdot d\mathbf A = M\,\cI{I}$$

Faraday: change makes an EMF

A voltage appears around the loop only while the flux changes, because the current changes or something moves. A strong but steady field gives nothing.

$$\mathcal E = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt}$$

Lenz: the induced current pushes back

The EMF drives a current \(\cI{I_\text{ind}} = \mathcal E/R\). Its own field opposes the change in flux, so the force on the magnet opposes its velocity. The kinetic energy lost becomes heat.

$$\cF{\mathbf F}\cdot\cv{\mathbf v} = -\cI{I_\text{ind}}^2 R \le 0$$

Ten times slower

The EMF peak is ten times smaller but lasts ten times longer. The area under \(\mathcal E(t)\) is the total change in flux, whatever the speed.

Flip the magnet

South pole first: the flux grows the other way, so the EMF and the current change sign. The force still opposes the motion.

More turns

Each turn collects the same flux change: four times the turns, four times the EMF.

Open circuit

The EMF is still there (it is a voltage across the gap), but no current flows: no induced field, no braking force.

Move it yourself

Drag the magnet in and out. Fast moves give large EMFs; the sign tells whether the flux is growing or shrinking.

Held still

Maximum flux, zero EMF: only a change in flux induces anything.

Magnetic braking

The induced current pushes back on the falling magnet. It falls more slowly than in free fall, and its lost kinetic energy heats the coil.

Open circuit

No current, no braking: the magnet falls freely at \(g\).

Lower resistance

Five times less resistance, five times the current for the same EMF: the magnet drifts down almost at a constant, slow speed (as in an eddy-current brake).

The induced electric field

An alternating current makes a changing field: nothing moves, yet the ring sees an EMF. The changing magnetic field is surrounded by a circulating electric field, even in empty space; the ring only gives its charges a path. This is how a transformer works.

$$\oint \cE{\mathbf E}\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt}, \qquad \mathcal E = -M\frac{d\cI{I}}{dt}$$

Double frequency

The current changes twice as fast, so \(dI/dt\) and the EMF double.

Direct current

A steady current gives a steady flux: zero EMF in the ring.

Further away

Less of the coil's field passes through the ring: smaller \(M\), smaller EMF.

About the model

Sources. The coil is \(N\) coaxial rings of radius \(R\) over length \(L\). A helix is drawn, but its small axial current component is neglected. The bar magnet is an Amperian cylinder: 12 rings carrying \((B_r/\mu_0)\,l_m/12\) each. The probe is one ring. Every ring stays parallel to the coil.

Field. Each ring's field and vector potential \(A_\varphi\) are the exact closed-form Biot–Savart result, using complete elliptic integrals computed by the arithmetic–geometric mean. Fields are tabulated on an \((\rho, z)\) grid: 160 asinh-spaced radial nodes, with axial nodes a divisor of the turn pitch, at most 3 mm. A strided prefix sum adds the turns, and bilinear interpolation fills in between nodes.

Induction. The model is quasi-static and resistive: \(I_\text{ind} = \mathcal E/R\), and self-inductance is neglected (valid while \(\omega L \ll R\)). The driven coil is an ideal current source. The flux uses reciprocity, \(\Phi = M I\), with \(M = \oint \mathbf A\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell\): exact \(2\pi r A_\varphi\) when coaxial, 48-point trapezoid otherwise. \(\nabla M\) is a central difference (\(h = 0.2\) mm).

Field lines. RK4 on \(\mathbf B/|\mathbf B|\) with step-doubling error control (tolerance 0.2 mm). Seeds sit on rings of equal flux in the source's mid-plane, so line density follows \(|\mathbf B|\). Colours span the 2nd–97th percentile of \(|\mathbf B|\) along the lines. Particles move along the lines with speed \(\propto |\mathbf B|\), capped at 3× the reference value near the wires. Charge carriers drift along the wire with speed \(\propto I\).

What is drawn. Field lines and particles show the applied field: the driven coil, or the magnet. The induced current's own field is much weaker. It is shown by the induced-B arrow and the moving charge carriers, and it is included in the slice, the arrow grid and every number.

Magnet drop. \(m\ddot z = -mg + F_z\), integrated by RK4 at \(\Delta t = 0.5\) ms.
