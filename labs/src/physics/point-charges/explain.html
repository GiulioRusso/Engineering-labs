Point charges

Drag the charges. Field lines start on positive charges and end on negative ones; the coloured slice is the field strength, the dark rings are equipotentials.

Coulomb's law and superposition

Each charge makes a field pointing away from it (if positive) and fading as \(1/r^2\). Several charges: add the arrows.

$$\cE{\mathbf E}(\mathbf r) = \sum_i \frac{q_i}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\mathbf r - \mathbf r_i}{|\mathbf r - \mathbf r_i|^3}$$

The number of lines leaving a charge is drawn in proportion to its size, so the line density follows the field.

Equipotentials cross the field at right angles

The potential is the energy per coulomb. Moving along an equipotential costs no work, so the field has no component along it.

$$V(\mathbf r) = \sum_i \frac{q_i}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 |\mathbf r - \mathbf r_i|}, \qquad \cE{\mathbf E} = -\nabla V$$

Gauss's law

Count the field flowing out of any closed surface: it only depends on the charge inside. Charges outside send in as much as they let out.

$$\oint \cE{\mathbf E}\cdot d\mathbf A = \frac{Q_\text{inside}}{\varepsilon_0}$$
