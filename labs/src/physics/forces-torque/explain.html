Forces and torque

Forces add as arrows

Several forces on one body act like a single force, their vector sum. Place them tip to tail: the arrow from the first tail to the last tip is the resultant. If they close into a loop, the body is in equilibrium.

$$\cF{\mathbf F_\text{net}} = \sum_i \cF{\mathbf F_i} = m\,\mathbf a$$

Torque turns

A force far from the pivot and at right angles to the bar turns it hardest. Pushing along the bar does nothing.

$$\tau = r\,F\sin\alpha, \qquad \tau = I\,\ddot\varphi, \qquad I_\text{bar} = \tfrac13 M L^2$$

Buoyancy: the weight of the displaced liquid

Pressure grows with depth, so the liquid pushes harder on the bottom of the block than on its top. The difference is the buoyant force, the weight of the liquid displaced (Archimedes). A block floats where that equals its own weight.

$$p = p_0 + \rho_f g h, \qquad F_B = \rho_f g V_\text{sub}, \qquad \frac{d}{s} = \frac{\rho_b}{\rho_f}$$
