Forces add as arrows

Several forces on one body act like a single force, their vector sum. Place them tip to tail: the arrow from the first tail to the last tip is the resultant, and it sets the acceleration. Drag the force tips.

$$\cF{\mathbf F_\text{net}} = \sum_i \cF{\mathbf F_i} = m\,\mathbf a$$

Along one line

10 N to the right and 4 N to the left: forces on one line just add with signs, net 6 N to the right.

At right angles

12 N and 9 N at 90° give \(\sqrt{12^2 + 9^2} = 15\) N, not 21 N: perpendicular forces add like the sides of a right triangle.

Equilibrium

Three 12 N forces 120° apart close into a triangle: the net force is zero, so the puck does not accelerate (it can still move at constant velocity).

Torque turns

What turns the bar is not the force alone but the force times its lever arm: the distance from the pivot, times the part of the force at right angles to the bar.

$$\tau = r\,F\sin\alpha, \qquad \tau = I\,\ddot\varphi, \qquad I_\text{bar} = \tfrac13 M L^2$$

At the end

Largest \(r\), force at 90°: the most torque. That is why door handles are far from the hinges.

Near the pivot

Same force, a tenth of the lever arm: a tenth of the torque.

Along the bar

\(\alpha = 0\): the force pulls straight through the pivot and has no lever arm. Zero torque however strong it is.

At 30°

\(\sin 30° = \tfrac12\): only half the force is at right angles to the bar.

Buoyancy: the weight of the displaced liquid

Pressure grows with depth, so the liquid pushes harder on the bottom of the block than on its top. The difference is the weight of the liquid displaced (Archimedes). The block floats where that equals its own weight: the submerged fraction is the density ratio.

$$p = p_0 + \rho_f g h, \qquad F_B = \rho_f g V_\text{sub}, \qquad \frac{d}{s} = \frac{\rho_b}{\rho_f}$$

Ice

\(917/1000\): 92 % of an iceberg is under water.

Sea water

Denser water (1025 kg/m³) lifts more: 89 % under.

Same density

Fully submerged, buoyancy exactly balances weight: it floats at any depth (neutral buoyancy, like a fish or a submarine).

Denser than water

Even fully under, the displaced water weighs less than the block: it sinks.
