Motion, momentum and energy

Three motions, three conservation stories. The green arrow is the velocity, the violet one the acceleration (lengths are scaled to stay readable).

Projectile: two independent motions

Without air, nothing pushes sideways, so the horizontal speed never changes; vertically, gravity removes the same speed every second. Kinetic energy turns into height and back, and their sum stays fixed.

$$x = v_0\cos\theta\, t, \quad y = h_0 + v_0\sin\theta\, t - \tfrac12 g t^2, \qquad \tfrac12 m v^2 + m g y = \text{const}$$

With air drag the total energy only goes down: the missing part has heated the air.

Circular motion: always falling inward

At constant speed the velocity still changes direction, so there is an acceleration, pointing to the centre.

$$a = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r, \qquad v = \omega r$$

Collisions: momentum always, energy sometimes

During the impact the two carts push on each other equally and oppositely, so their total momentum cannot change. Kinetic energy survives only if the collision is elastic (\(e = 1\)).

$$m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2, \qquad v_2 - v_1 = e\,(u_1 - u_2)$$
