Lagrange points

Ride along with two bodies orbiting each other: in that turning frame they stand still. A small third body feels their gravity plus the centrifugal push outward. The surface shows that combined potential: deep wells at the two bodies, and five places where the forces balance.

The effective potential

$$\Omega(x,y) = \tfrac12(x^2+y^2) + \frac{1-\mu}{r_1} + \frac{\mu}{r_2}, \qquad \ddot x - 2\dot y = \Omega_x,\quad \ddot y + 2\dot x = \Omega_y$$

Units: the separation is 1 and one orbit takes \(2\pi\). The \(2\dot y,\ 2\dot x\) terms are the Coriolis force.

Five equilibria

L1, L2 and L3 lie on the line through the two bodies: they are saddles, and a body placed there drifts away. L4 and L5 sit at the tips of two equilateral triangles: they are tops of hills, yet they are stable. The Coriolis force turns a body that rolls downhill back around the point, as long as the small mass is less than about 4 % of the total.

$$\mu < \tfrac12\left(1 - \sqrt{23/27}\right) \approx 0.0385$$

The Jacobi constant

In the turning frame energy is not conserved, but this combination is. It keeps a body inside the regions it can reach.

$$C = 2\Omega - v^2$$
