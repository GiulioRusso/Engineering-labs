Orbital transfers

The Hohmann transfer

To climb from one circular orbit to another, burn forward once to stretch the orbit into an ellipse that touches the target, then burn again at the far end to round it off. It is the cheapest two-burn transfer.

$$a_t = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}, \quad \Delta v_1 = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_1}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2r_2}{r_1 + r_2}} - 1\right), \quad t = \pi\sqrt{\frac{a_t^3}{\mu}}$$

Going further to arrive cheaper

The bi-elliptic transfer first overshoots to a distant apoapsis \(r_b\), where the spacecraft is slow and changing the orbit is cheap, then falls back to the target. It saves fuel only when the target is more than about 12 times further out, and it takes much longer.

The rocket equation

Each burn costs propellant exponentially: the rocket must also accelerate the fuel it has not burned yet.

$$\Delta v = I_{sp}\, g_0 \ln\frac{m_0}{m_f}, \qquad m_\text{prop} = m_\text{dry}\left(e^{\Delta v / I_{sp} g_0} - 1\right)$$
