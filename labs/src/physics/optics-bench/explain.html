Optics

Lenses and mirrors form images

Three easy rays find the image: one parallel to the axis (it bends through the focus), one through the centre (it goes straight), one through the near focus (it leaves parallel). Where they meet is the image; if they only meet when extended backwards, the image is virtual.

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}, \qquad m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}, \qquad f_\text{mirror} = \frac{R}{2}$$

Refraction: Snell's law

Light is slower in glass or water (by the factor \(n\)), so a ray crossing at an angle bends toward the normal going in and away from it coming out. From the dense side beyond the critical angle nothing gets out: total internal reflection.

$$n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2, \qquad \sin\theta_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1}$$

Two slits: light as a wave

Waves from two openings overlap. Where their paths differ by a whole number of wavelengths they add (bright); half a wavelength, they cancel (dark).

$$r_2 - r_1 = m\lambda \;\Rightarrow\; \text{bright}, \qquad \Delta y \approx \frac{\lambda L}{d}$$

This bench uses ripple-tank wavelengths (centimetres) so you can see the waves themselves.
