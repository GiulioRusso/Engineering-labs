Electromagnetic waves

Light, radio and microwaves are the same thing: an electric field and a magnetic field that keep each other going while travelling at \(c\). Here the wave runs along \(x\), with \(\mathbf E\) vertical and \(\mathbf B\) horizontal.

E and B together

In a travelling wave \(\mathbf E\) and \(\mathbf B\) are in phase, at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel. Their ratio is fixed.

$$\frac{|\cE{\mathbf E}|}{|\cB{\mathbf B}|} = \frac{c}{n}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{c}{n f}, \qquad \mathbf S = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\cE{\mathbf E}\times\cB{\mathbf B}$$

Reflection at a dielectric

At normal incidence part of the wave bounces back and part goes on, shorter and slower. The fractions of power add up to one.

$$r = \frac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1 + n_2}, \quad t = \frac{2 n_1}{n_1 + n_2}, \qquad R + T = r^2 + \frac{n_2}{n_1}t^2 = 1$$

Standing waves

A perfect conductor reflects everything with \(\mathbf E\) inverted, so \(\mathbf E = 0\) on its surface. The incoming and reflected waves add to a pattern that does not travel: nodes every half wavelength.

$$E(x, t) = 2E_0 \sin(kx)\sin(\omega t)$$
