The electromagnetic wave

What you are looking at

A plane wave travelling along z: E (one colour) and B (the other) at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel. In interface mode the wave meets a second medium and part of it is reflected.

A changing E makes a B, a changing B makes an E: each sustains the other and the pair travels at the speed of light, with no medium needed. E and B are in phase, perpendicular, and their ratio is fixed. At a boundary between materials the wave slows down (refractive index n) and part of it bounces back.

$$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}}, \qquad B = \frac{E}{c}, \qquad \lambda = \frac{c}{f}, \qquad I = \frac{E_0^2}{2\eta_0}, \qquad R = \left(\frac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1 + n_2}\right)^2$$
  1. Higher frequency, shorter wavelength: radio (metres), microwaves (cm), light (0.5 µm).
  2. Intensity grows with E₀²: double the field, four times the power.
  3. A conductor reflects everything: incident and reflected waves form a standing wave with E = 0 at the surface.

Worked example

1 GHz: λ = 30 cm. E₀ = 10 V/m: B₀ = 33 nT, intensity 0.13 W/m². Air to glass (n = 1.5): 4 % of the light is reflected at each surface.

Common mistake

Drawing E and B a quarter cycle apart (like in an LC circuit): in a travelling wave they peak together.

E and B together

In a travelling wave \(\mathbf E\) and \(\mathbf B\) are in phase, at right angles to each other and to the direction of travel. Their ratio is fixed; the energy flows along \(\mathbf E\times\mathbf B\).

$$\frac{|\cE{\mathbf E}|}{|\cB{\mathbf B}|} = c, \qquad \lambda = \frac{c}{f}, \qquad \mathbf S = \frac{1}{\mu_0}\cE{\mathbf E}\times\cB{\mathbf B}$$

Higher frequency

\(\lambda = c/f\): 10 GHz gives 3 cm instead of 30 cm. The scene always shows three wavelengths; read \(\lambda\) in the readouts.

Double amplitude

\(\mathbf B\) doubles with \(\mathbf E\); the power carried goes up four times, as \(E_0^2\).

Reflection at a dielectric

At normal incidence part of the wave bounces back and part goes on, shorter and slower (\(\lambda/n\)). The fractions of power add up to one.

$$r = \frac{n_1 - n_2}{n_1 + n_2}, \qquad R = r^2, \qquad R + T = 1$$

No contrast

\(n_2 = n_1\): \(r = 0\), the wave does not see the boundary.

A dense material

\(r = -3/5\): 36 % of the power is reflected, and the transmitted wave is four times shorter.

Standing waves

A perfect conductor reflects everything with \(\mathbf E\) inverted, so \(\mathbf E = 0\) on its surface. Incoming and reflected waves add up to a pattern that does not travel: nodes every half wavelength.

$$E(x, t) = 2E_0 \sin(kx)\sin(\omega t)$$

Into glass

\(r = (1 - 1.5)/(1 + 1.5) = -0.2\): \(R = 4\%\) of the power comes back, 96 % goes on with a wavelength 1.5 times shorter.

Standing wave

The mirror sends everything back with \(\mathbf E\) inverted. Incoming and reflected waves add up to a pattern that does not travel: nodes every \(\lambda/2\), where \(\mathbf E\) is always zero.
