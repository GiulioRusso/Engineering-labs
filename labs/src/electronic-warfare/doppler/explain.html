The Doppler effect: squeezed wavefronts

What you are looking at

The radar sends a continuous wave: orange arcs are crests travelling out, blue arcs are crests reflected by the aircraft travelling back. Bottom: the transmitted wave (grey) and the received wave (blue) at the radar. The aircraft's speed is exaggerated enormously so the effect is visible.

Each crest meets the approaching aircraft a little sooner than the one before, and the aircraft itself has moved closer when it reflects the next one. So the reflected crests are closer together: shorter wavelength, higher frequency. Moving away, they are stretched.

$$f_r = f_0\,\frac{c + v}{c - v} \;\approx\; f_0\left(1 + \frac{2v}{c}\right) \quad\Rightarrow\quad f_d = f_r - f_0 = \frac{2 v f_0}{c} = \frac{2v}{\lambda}$$

Why the factor 2

The shift happens twice: once when the moving aircraft receives the wave (it runs into the crests), once when it re-transmits it as a moving source. Each contributes \(v/c\).

Worked example

X-band, \(f_0 = 10\) GHz (λ = 3 cm), aircraft closing at 300 m/s: \(f_d = 2 \cdot 300 / 0.03 = 20\) kHz. A shift of 2 parts per million of the carrier — tiny, but easy to measure because it accumulates phase pulse after pulse. Rule of thumb at X-band: 66.7 Hz per m/s.

Common mistakes

Only the radial component counts: an aircraft crossing perpendicular to the beam has zero Doppler however fast it flies. And the sign convention: here positive means closing (higher frequency).

Try it

Set the speed to 0: the spacing is the same both ways. Reverse it: the blue arcs spread out and the received wave slows down.

Moving away

Opening at 300 m/s: \(f_d = -20\) kHz, the reflected crests are further apart than the transmitted ones.

At rest

No motion, no shift: the received wave has the transmitted frequency. A stationary building or hill gives exactly this, which is how Doppler radars reject ground clutter.

Mach 3

1000 m/s → 66.7 kHz at X-band. The shift is still only 7 ppm of the carrier.

Pulse-Doppler: range and velocity together

What you are looking at

Left: the scene (radar at the bottom, target and its velocity arrow). Centre: the range profile — a peak at the target's range, as wide as the resolution \(c\tau/2\). Right: the Doppler spectrum — a peak at the target's radial velocity.

Range comes from the echo delay within one pulse ("fast time"). Velocity comes from how the echo phase advances from pulse to pulse ("slow time"): over N pulses that is a sampled sine at \(f_d\), and an FFT across the pulses finds it.

$$\Delta R = \frac{c\tau}{2}, \qquad \Delta v = \frac{\lambda\,\text{PRF}}{2N}, \qquad v_u = \pm\frac{\lambda\,\text{PRF}}{4}$$

Step by step

  1. Fire N pulses, one every PRI.
  2. For each range cell, take the N complex samples (one per pulse).
  3. A target at \(v\) makes them rotate by \(2\pi f_d\,\text{PRI}\) each pulse.
  4. FFT the N samples: the peak bin gives \(f_d\), hence \(v = \lambda f_d / 2\).

Worked example

10 GHz, PRF 10 kHz, 64 pulses: \(\Delta v = 0.03 \cdot 10^4 / 128 = 2.3\) m/s, \(v_u = \pm 75\) m/s. A target at 300 m/s exceeds \(v_u\) and wraps to a wrong velocity.

Common mistake

Thinking the Doppler spectrum is measured inside one pulse. A 1 µs pulse at 20 kHz Doppler sees only 0.02 of a cycle — the information comes from the sequence of pulses.

Try it

Quadruple N: the Doppler peak gets four times narrower. Push the speed past ±75 m/s and watch the peak reappear at the other side.

More pulses

256 pulses: 0.6 m/s resolution — at the cost of 25 ms in one beam direction.

Velocity wrap

120 m/s at ±75 m/s unambiguous: reported at 120 − 150 = −30 m/s. The phase advance per pulse exceeded half a turn, so it looks like a smaller turn the other way.

S-band

3 GHz: λ = 10 cm, so the same speed gives a third of the Doppler, but \(v_u\) is three times larger at the same PRF.

The range–Doppler map

What you are looking at

Left: the radar's image — apparent range across, radial velocity up, brightness = echo strength (dB). Each target is a spot, its size the resolution cell \(c\tau/2 \times \Delta v\). The thin cross-shaped ripples are the range and Doppler sidelobes. Right: the scene seen from above.

Do the FFT over slow time for every range cell and stack the results: a 2-D picture where targets that share a range cell can still be told apart by velocity, and vice versa. The whole picture is periodic: everything is folded into \(0 \ldots R_u\) and \(-v_u \ldots v_u\).

Worked example

PRF 10 kHz: \(R_u = 15\) km, \(v_u = \pm 75\) m/s. A target at 21 km is drawn at 6 km. A target at −20 m/s sits below the zero-velocity line.

Common mistake

Reading the map as true range and velocity. It shows apparent ones; resolving the truth needs several PRFs.

Try it

Put two targets at the same range with different speeds: they separate vertically. Lower the PRF and watch the velocity window shrink and the spots wrap around.

One range cell, two speeds

A and B both at 8 km but 15 m/s apart: impossible to split by range, obvious by Doppler.

Range folding

C at 21 km with \(R_u = 15\) km is drawn at 6 km, right in the middle of the picture.

Low PRF

3 kHz: \(R_u = 50\) km (no range folding) but \(v_u = \pm 22\) m/s: A at 40 m/s wraps to −5 m/s.

The PRF dilemma

What you are looking at

Left: reported range against true range — a sawtooth. Right: reported velocity against true velocity — another sawtooth. The thick green parts are where the radar is honest; the dot is your target.

The PRF sets both limits, in opposite directions:

$$R_u = \frac{c}{2\,\text{PRF}}, \qquad v_u = \frac{\lambda\,\text{PRF}}{4}, \qquad R_u\, v_u = \frac{c\lambda}{8} = \text{const}$$

Raise the PRF and velocity becomes honest while range folds; lower it and the opposite. You cannot have both large: that is why radars talk of low PRF (range honest), high PRF (velocity honest) and medium PRF (both ambiguous, resolved with several PRFs).

Worked example

At 10 GHz the product is \(3\times10^8 \cdot 0.03 / 8 \approx 1.1\times10^6\) m²/s: with \(R_u = 15\) km you get \(v_u = 75\) m/s, never both 100 km and 300 m/s.

Try it

Drag the PRF and watch one green segment grow while the other shrinks.

Inside both limits

10 km and 40 m/s at 10 kHz: both honest.

High PRF

20 kHz: \(v_u = 150\) m/s (the 100 m/s target is honest) but \(R_u = 7.5\) km: 20 km reads as 5 km.

Low PRF

4 kHz: \(R_u = 37.5\) km but \(v_u = 30\) m/s: the 100 m/s target wraps.
