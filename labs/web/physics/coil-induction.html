How it works

Click a section to highlight what it describes in the scene.

1 · One loop of current

Every short piece of wire adds a little field, perpendicular to both the wire and the line to the point (Biot–Savart). Summed around a ring, the field threads through the middle along the axis and curls back around the wire outside.

Bz(z)=μ0IR22 (R2+z2)3/2\cB{B_z}(z) = \frac{\mu_0 \cI{I} R^2}{2\,(R^2+z^2)^{3/2}}

Far away the ring looks like a magnetic dipole and its field fades as 1/r31/r^3.

2 · Many loops: the solenoid

Side by side, the rings' fields add up inside and nearly cancel outside. In a long coil the field inside is uniform and depends only on the turns per metre n=N/Ln = N/L, not on the radius.

B≈μ0 n I\cB{B} \approx \mu_0\, n\, \cI{I}

Near the ends the lines spread out (fringing): at an end of a long coil the field is half the central value.

3 · Flux through a loop

Flux counts how much field passes through a surface bounded by a loop. It is linear in the source current, with the mutual inductance MM as the constant: a geometric number set by the shapes and the positions.

Φ=∫SB⋅dA=∮A⋅dℓ=M I\Phi = \int_S \cB{\mathbf B}\cdot d\mathbf A = \oint \mathbf A\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell = M\,\cI{I}

4 · Faraday: change makes an EMF

A voltage appears around the loop only while the flux changes, because the current changes or the loop moves. A strong but steady field gives nothing.

E=−dΦdt=−MdIdt−I ∇M⋅v\mathcal E = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -M\frac{d\cI{I}}{dt} - \cI{I}\,\nabla M\cdot\cv{\mathbf v}

Pushing the same magnet in faster gives a bigger EMF for a shorter time. The area under E(t)\mathcal E(t) is the total change in flux, whatever the speed.

5 · Lenz: the induced current pushes back

The EMF drives a current Iind=E/R\cI{I_\text{ind}} = \mathcal E/R through the circuit. The minus sign means its own field opposes the change in flux, so the force on the moving magnet opposes its velocity. The kinetic energy lost becomes heat in the resistance.

F=Is Iind ∇M,F⋅v=−Iind2R≤0\cF{\mathbf F} = \cI{I_s}\,\cI{I_\text{ind}}\,\nabla M, \qquad \cF{\mathbf F}\cdot\cv{\mathbf v} = -\cI{I_\text{ind}}^2 R \le 0

6 · The induced electric field

A changing magnetic field is surrounded by a circulating electric field, even in empty space with no wire there. The wire only gives its charges a path to follow.

∮E⋅dℓ=−dΦdt,E=−∂A∂t\oint \cE{\mathbf E}\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt}, \qquad \cE{\mathbf E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf A}{\partial t}

About the model

Sources. The coil is NN coaxial rings of radius RR over length LL. A helix is drawn, but its small axial current component is neglected. The bar magnet is an Amperian cylinder: 12 rings carrying (Br/μ0) lm/12(B_r/\mu_0)\,l_m/12 each. The probe is one ring. Every ring stays parallel to the coil.

Field. Each ring's field and vector potential AφA_\varphi are the exact closed-form Biot–Savart result, using complete elliptic integrals computed by the arithmetic–geometric mean. Fields are tabulated on an (ρ,z)(\rho, z) grid: 160 asinh-spaced radial nodes, with axial nodes a divisor of the turn pitch, at most 3 mm. A strided prefix sum adds the turns, and bilinear interpolation fills in between nodes.

Induction. The model is quasi-static and resistive: Iind=E/RI_\text{ind} = \mathcal E/R, and self-inductance is neglected (valid while ωL≪R\omega L \ll R). The driven coil is an ideal current source. The flux uses reciprocity, Φ=MI\Phi = M I, with M=∮A⋅dℓM = \oint \mathbf A\cdot d\boldsymbol\ell: exact 2πrAφ2\pi r A_\varphi when coaxial, 48-point trapezoid otherwise. ∇M\nabla M is a central difference (h=0.2h = 0.2 mm).

Field lines. RK4 on B/∣B∣\mathbf B/|\mathbf B| with step-doubling error control (tolerance 0.2 mm). Seeds sit on rings of equal flux in the source's mid-plane, so line density follows ∣B∣|\mathbf B|. Colours span the 2nd–97th percentile of ∣B∣|\mathbf B| along the lines. Particles move along the lines with speed ∝∣B∣\propto |\mathbf B|, capped at 3× the reference value near the wires. Charge carriers drift along the wire with speed ∝I\propto I.

What is drawn. Field lines and particles show the applied field: the driven coil, or the magnet. The induced current's own field is much weaker. It is shown by the induced-B arrow and the moving charge carriers, and it is included in the slice, the arrow grid and every number.

Magnet drop. mz¨=−mg+Fzm\ddot z = -mg + F_z, integrated by RK4 at Δt=0.5\Delta t = 0.5 ms.
