Cutting a cone

What you are looking at

A double cone (two nappes meeting at the tip) and a flat plane slicing through it; the orange curve is where they meet. Drag to rotate.

The Greeks found that circles, ellipses, parabolas and hyperbolas are all the same thing: the shape you get when you slice a cone. Only the angle of the cut changes. Flat: a circle. Tilted a little: an ellipse. Tilted exactly as steeply as the cone's side: the curve never closes — a parabola. Steeper still: the plane cuts both nappes — a hyperbola with two branches.

$$e = \frac{\sin\beta}{\cos\alpha}: \quad e = 0 \text{ circle}, \; e < 1 \text{ ellipse}, \; e = 1 \text{ parabola}, \; e > 1 \text{ hyperbola}$$

Worked example

Cone of half-angle 30°: a plane tilted 25° gives an ellipse (e = 0.49); at 60° a parabola; at 80° a hyperbola (e = 1.14).

Common mistake

Thinking the parabola is "half an ellipse": it is the boundary case, open for ever.

Try it

Slowly raise the tilt from 0: watch the closed curve stretch, open at exactly 90° − α, and jump to the other nappe.

A flat cut

Perpendicular to the axis: a circle.

Parallel to the side

The plane never meets the far side of the cone: the curve runs off to infinity, a parabola.

A steep cut

Both nappes are cut: two separate branches.

Focus and directrix

What you are looking at

A conic with its focus (red dot at the origin) and directrix (dashed line). The orange lines join a moving point to the focus and to the directrix.

Every conic has a single definition: the points whose distance to a fixed point (focus) is e times their distance to a fixed line (directrix). The number e — the eccentricity — decides the shape.

$$PF = e\cdot PD, \qquad r(\theta) = \frac{p}{1 + e\cos\theta}$$

Worked example

Planet orbits are ellipses with the Sun at one focus (Kepler): Earth e = 0.017 (almost a circle), Halley's comet e = 0.97. A probe escaping the Sun follows a hyperbola (e > 1).

Common mistake

Putting the Sun at the centre of an elliptical orbit: it sits at a focus.

e → 0

The directrix runs away to infinity and the curve becomes a circle around the focus.

e = 1

Every point is exactly as far from the focus as from the line.

e = 1.5

Closer to the line than to the focus is impossible on this branch: it opens away, a hyperbola.

The reflection property

What you are looking at

Rays leaving the focus (green) and bouncing off the curve as off a mirror.

The shape of a conic makes every bounce do the same thing. A parabola sends every ray from its focus out parallel — and, reversed, gathers a parallel beam into the focus: satellite dishes, telescopes, headlights. An ellipse sends every ray from one focus to the other: whisper at one focus of an elliptical room and you are heard at the other.

$$\text{angle of incidence} = \text{angle of reflection, measured from the normal}$$

Common mistake

Placing the receiver of a dish at its centre of curvature instead of at the focus (half that distance).

A dish

All rays leave parallel: a perfectly collimated beam.

Focus to focus

Every ray from the right focus passes through the left focus.
