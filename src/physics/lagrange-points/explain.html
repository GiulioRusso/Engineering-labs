The effective potential

Ride along with two bodies orbiting each other: in that turning frame they stand still. A small third body feels their gravity plus the centrifugal push outward. The surface shows the combined potential, with five places where the forces balance.

$$\Omega(x,y) = \tfrac12(x^2+y^2) + \frac{1-\mu}{r_1} + \frac{\mu}{r_2}, \qquad \ddot x - 2\dot y = \Omega_x,\quad \ddot y + 2\dot x = \Omega_y$$

The Lagrange points

What you are looking at

Two bodies (e.g. Earth and Moon) seen in a frame that rotates with them; the surface is the effective potential (gravity + centrifugal), with its five flat points L1–L5. A small body is released near the chosen point and its path traced.

In the rotating frame, a small body feels the two gravities and a centrifugal push outward. At five places these three balance: it can stay at rest relative to both bodies. L1, L2, L3 sit on the line through the bodies and are saddles: a nudge and it drifts away. L4 and L5 form equilateral triangles with the bodies; they are hilltops of the potential, yet stable — the Coriolis force bends a drifting body back around (if the small/large mass ratio μ < 0.0385).

$$\Omega(x, y) = \frac{1 - \mu}{r_1} + \frac{\mu}{r_2} + \frac{x^2 + y^2}{2}, \qquad r_{L1,L2} \approx a\left(\frac{\mu}{3}\right)^{1/3}$$

Worked example

Sun–Earth: L1 and L2 are 1.5 million km from Earth — SOHO watches the Sun from L1, the James Webb telescope sits at L2. Sun–Jupiter L4/L5 hold thousands of Trojan asteroids.

Common mistake

Thinking L4/L5 are stable because they are valleys: they are maxima of the potential; stability comes from the Coriolis force.

Try it

Start near L1 with a tiny offset and watch it leave; start near L4 and watch it circle around (a tadpole orbit).

L5

The mirror image of L4, 60° behind the small body: just as stable.

Jupiter Trojans

Thousands of asteroids circle the Sun–Jupiter L4 and L5 points, trapped there for billions of years.

Too heavy

With \(\mu = 0.06\), above the Routh limit, the Coriolis force can no longer hold the body: it spirals away.

L1, L2, L3: saddles

On the line through the two bodies: along the line they are hilltops, across it valleys. A nudged body drifts away, so spacecraft there need small corrections.

L2

Beyond the small body. Sun–Earth L2, 1.5 million km out, hosts JWST: Sun, Earth and Moon all stay behind its shield.

L3

On the far side of the big body. The slowest to drift away, but still unstable.

The Jacobi constant

In the turning frame energy is not conserved, but this combination is. It keeps a body inside the regions it can reach.

$$C = 2\Omega - v^2$$

L1

Between the two bodies, where their pulls and the centrifugal push balance. Along the line it is a hilltop: a tiny nudge grows and the body drifts away. Sun–Earth L1 hosts solar observatories (SOHO).
