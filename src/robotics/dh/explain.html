Forward kinematics and the DH convention

Forward kinematics answers: given the joint angles, where is the hand? Each link is a rigid body, so the pose of each frame relative to the one before is a homogeneous transform; chain them and you have the hand. The Denavit–Hartenberg convention makes every link transform the same shape, fixed by only four numbers: put each frame’s z axis along a joint axis, then go from one frame to the next by a rotation θ about z, a slide d along z, a slide a along the new x, and a twist α about it. For a revolute joint θ is the joint variable; a, α and d are fixed by the link’s geometry and make up the DH table.

$$A_i^{i-1}(q_i) = R_z(\vartheta_i)\,T_z(d_i)\,T_x(a_i)\,R_x(\alpha_i), \qquad T_n^0(\mathbf q) = A_1^0(q_1)\,A_2^1(q_2)\cdots A_n^{n-1}(q_n)$$
  1. z_{i−1} along the axis of joint i.
  2. x_i along the common normal between z_{i−1} and z_i; a_i is its length, α_i the twist between the two axes.
  3. d_i: offset along z_{i−1}; θ_i: angle about z_{i−1}, the joint variable.
  4. Multiply the n transforms: position = last column, orientation = the 3×3 block.

Worked example

Anthropomorphic arm, table (a, α, d) = (0, 90°, 0.40), (0.45, 0, 0), (0.40, 0, 0). Stretched out (q₂ = q₃ = 0) the tip is at (0.45 + 0.40, 0, 0.40) = (0.85, 0, 0.40). With q₂ = 30°, q₃ = −60°: x = 0.45 cos 30° + 0.40 cos(−30°) = 0.736, z = 0.40 + 0.45 sin 30° − 0.40 sin 30° = 0.425.

Common mistake

Mixing up which frame a DH row belongs to: row i uses θᵢ and dᵢ along z_{i−1} (the previous frame) but aᵢ and αᵢ along xᵢ (the new one).

Try it

Highlight frame 1 and change q₁: frame 1 turns about z₀, and everything beyond it turns along.

Turning the base

Joint 1 turns about the vertical axis: the whole arm swings round, the tip keeps its height (0.425 m) and distance from the axis.

Elbow down

q₂ = −20°, q₃ = 60°: a different posture; the tip moves to (0.729, 0, 0.503). For most positions there are two answers, elbow up and elbow down: inverse kinematics has to choose.

Stretched out

All in line: the tip at 0.85 m, as far as it can go. Here the arm cannot move the tip further out: a singular configuration, the edge of the workspace.

Frame 1

Row 1: a = 0, α = 90°, d = 0.40 m. Frame 1 sits 0.40 m up the base axis (the shoulder) and is twisted 90° about x, so its z axis lies horizontal: the shoulder joint’s axis.

A 7-joint arm: the Kinova Jaco2

The Jaco2 of the course project has seven revolute joints with a very regular DH table: every a is zero and every twist is 90°, so consecutive axes are perpendicular, with offsets d along them (0.2755 m at the base, −0.41 m upper arm, −0.31 m forearm, 0.26 m to the gripper). Seven joints for a six-dimensional task (position and orientation) leave one spare degree of freedom: the elbow can swing round the line from shoulder to wrist while the hand stays still. The project uses this arm for CLIK tracking of a square and for impedance control against a surface.

$$\mathbf a = \mathbf 0, \quad \boldsymbol\alpha = (90°, 90°, 90°, 90°, 90°, 90°, 0), \quad \mathbf d = (0.2755,\ 0,\ -0.41,\ -0.0098,\ -0.3111,\ 0,\ 0.2638)\ \text{m}$$
  1. Same recipe: seven A matrices, multiplied in order.
  2. The geometric Jacobian has 6 rows and 7 columns: at best rank 6, one direction of joint motion moves nothing at the hand (the null space).
  3. At some postures links line up and the rank drops: a singularity.

Worked example

The project’s home posture q₀ = (77, −17, 0, 43, −94, 77, 71)°: the gripper at (0.212, −0.264, 0.498) m, Jacobian rank 6.

Common mistake

Using the robot maker’s joint angles directly in a DH model. The Kinova convention differs from DH by offsets and signs; the project converts with mask_q_DH2Jaco and mask_q_Jaco2DH.

Try it

Highlight joint 3, tick “Sweep”: the elbow rolls, and the tip traces only a short arc.

All zeros

With this DH table, all joints at zero fold the arm onto itself: the tip ends 11 cm above the base and the Jacobian’s rank drops to 3. Half the directions of motion are lost: a strongly singular posture to keep away from.

The wrist

Joint 7 turns the gripper about its own axis: position unchanged, orientation (roll) changes. The last three joints form the wrist that sets orientation.

The redundant elbow

Joint 3 at 60° instead of 0: the elbow moves and the hand moves too. Moving the hand back to where it was, using the other joints, is exactly the null-space motion of a redundant arm (see Redundancy).

About the model

Standard DH convention (Siciliano et al., ch. 2.8); the Jaco2 table is the one in the course model (G. Antonelli, Introduction to Robotics, University of Cassino, 2022/23), as used by the Jaco2 project’s DirectKinematics.m. Tests: the geometric Jacobian of both arms matches finite differences of the forward kinematics, position (10⁻⁸) and angular velocity (10⁻⁶).
