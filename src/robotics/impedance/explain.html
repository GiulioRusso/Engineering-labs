Impedance control

What you are looking at

Left: a two-link arm seen from above, its tip resting against a compliant wall; the red dot is the desired point, pushed into the wall over a couple of seconds. Top right: how far past the wall the tip and the desired point are (mm). Bottom right: the contact force, with its predicted final value dashed. Below: the joint torques.

Position control in contact is dangerous: if the robot is told to be 5 cm inside a wall, a stiff position loop pushes harder and harder. Impedance control instead makes the tip behave like a mass on a spring and damper attached to the desired point. In free space nothing stretches the spring and the tip follows the desired point exactly. Against the wall, the spring stretches until its pull balances the wall’s push-back: the force is bounded and chosen through K_P. Two springs in series decide where the tip stops: whichever is softer gives way. This is the Jaco2 project’s task 4, pushing the gripper 20 cm into a compliant plane.

$$M_d\,\ddot{\tilde{\mathbf x}} + K_D\,\dot{\tilde{\mathbf x}} + K_P\,\tilde{\mathbf x} = \mathbf h_e, \quad \tilde{\mathbf x} = \mathbf x - \mathbf x_d; \qquad \text{at rest: } f = \frac{k\,K_P}{K_P + k}\,\Delta, \qquad \boldsymbol\tau = B(\mathbf q)\,\mathbf y + \mathbf n(\mathbf q, \dot{\mathbf q}) + J^\mathsf{T}\mathbf h_e$$
  1. Measure the contact force h_e (a force sensor at the wrist).
  2. Compute the tip acceleration the target impedance prescribes, and the joint acceleration y that produces it (J⁻¹, minus the J̇q̇ term).
  3. Inverse dynamics gives the torques; the measured force is cancelled so only the virtual one acts.

Worked example

K_P = 400 N/m, wall k = 2000 N/m, desired point 50 mm in: f = 2000 × 400 / 2400 × 0.05 = 16.7 N; the tip sinks 16.7/2000 = 8.3 mm into the wall and stays 41.7 mm short of the desired point.

Common mistake

Choosing the damping without the wall. In contact the spring is K_P + k, much stiffer than in free space: ζ = K_D/2√((K_P + k)M_d) falls, and the contact can ring. The project’s committed gains (M_d = 100, K_D = 100, K_P = 1) err the other way: ζ = 5 in free space and a time constant K_D/K_P = 100 s, too slow to settle in a 30 s run.

Try it

Sweep K_P from 10 to 10 000 N/m: the force rises from almost nothing to almost kΔ = 100 N.

Compliant

K_P = 100 N/m: the robot is the soft spring. 4.8 N and only 2.4 mm into the wall: a gentle touch, whatever the wall is made of.

Stiff

K_P = 5000 N/m, more than the wall: 71 N, 36 mm into the wall. The robot now behaves almost like position control, and the force depends on the environment.

A hard wall

k = 20 000 N/m: the tip barely sinks in (1 mm) and the force, 19.6 N, approaches K_P Δ = 20 N: with a compliant robot the force hardly depends on the wall.

A fast push

The desired point jumps in over 0.2 s: the tip arrives with speed and the force overshoots to 23 N before settling at 16.7.

No wall

In free space the impedance is never excited: the tip follows the desired point to within micrometres. Impedance control is position control until something pushes back.

About the model

Two links (1 m, 5 kg and 0.8 m, 3 kg) in a horizontal plane (no gravity), exact inverse-dynamics impedance control with the measured force (Siciliano et al., §9.3; the project’s main_progetto_4 implements the position part on the Jaco2). Wall: a linear spring acting along x only when penetrated, no friction. RK4 at 0.5 ms. Tests: final force equals kK_PΔ/(K_P + k) for compliant, medium and stiff gains (0.2 %); in free space the tip tracks the desired point within 10⁻⁶ m; the tip never crosses back and the force barely overshoots for a slow push.
