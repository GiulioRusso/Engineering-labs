Closed-loop inverse kinematics (CLIK)

What you are looking at

Left: a three-link arm whose tip follows a path (dashed: the reference; orange: where the tip actually went; faint arms: earlier postures). Top right: the tracking error in millimetres. Bottom right: the three joint angles. Below: how fast the joints turn. The path is the Jaco2 project’s: a 40 cm square, 3 s per side with a trapezoidal speed profile, short stops at the corners.

Inverse kinematics in closed form exists only for simple arms. The general method works with velocities instead: if the tip should move at ẋ_d, the joints should move at q̇ = J⁻¹ẋ_d. Integrating that alone would slowly drift, because each step is a little wrong; so the error between where the tip should be and where it is gets fed back with a gain K. The result is a loop in which the error decays exponentially, e(t) ∝ e^(−Kt), and the joint angles are found step by step, every Ts = 10 ms. When the arm has more joints than the task has coordinates, J is not square and the pseudo-inverse J⁺ takes the minimum-speed solution.

$$\dot{\mathbf q} = J^\dagger(\mathbf q)\,\big(\dot{\mathbf x}_d + K\,\mathbf e\big), \qquad \mathbf e = \mathbf x_d - \mathbf x_e, \qquad J^\dagger = J^\mathsf{T}(JJ^\mathsf{T})^{-1}, \qquad \mathbf q_{k+1} = \mathbf q_k + T_s\,\dot{\mathbf q}_k$$
  1. Forward kinematics: where is the tip now? Error e.
  2. Command: path speed (feedforward) plus K e (feedback).
  3. Map to joints with J⁺ (or Jᵀ, or the damped inverse).
  4. Integrate one step; repeat. In the project the resulting q is streamed to CoppeliaSim.

Worked example

Pseudo-inverse, K = 50 /s: largest error 0.021 mm, the same order as the project’s task 1 (0.008 mm with its Jaco2). With K = 5 the loop is ten times slower: 0.20 mm.

Common mistake

Making K as large as possible. With Euler steps of Ts, K·Ts must stay well below 1 (here 2 at most), or the discrete loop overshoots and goes unstable; and a real robot has motor limits.

Try it

Switch between J⁺ and Jᵀ and compare the scale of the error plot.

The transpose

q̇ = Jᵀ K e: no matrix to invert, never singular, but no feedforward of the path speed: the tip only moves because it is behind. 17 mm of lag on the square at K = 20 (higher gains go unstable with these steps).

Low gain

K = 5: the feedforward still does most of the work, so the error stays small (0.20 mm), but it is corrected ten times more slowly.

Position and angle

Now the gripper’s angle is held too (like the project’s task 2): three coordinates for three joints, J is square, no redundancy left. The orientation error stays below 10⁻³ rad and the joints move more (up to 0.7 rad/s) to keep the gripper level.

A circle

The circle starts at full speed: the first step has a 2 mm error, which the feedback removes within a fraction of a second; after that it stays at hundredths of a millimetre.

Out of reach

Starting nearly stretched, the square goes outward beyond the arm’s reach. As the arm straightens, JJᵀ becomes singular and J⁺ asks for joint speeds of thousands of rad/s: the simulation goes wild. A real robot would hit its limits or fault.

Damped least squares

J* = Jᵀ(JJᵀ + λ²I)⁻¹ with λ = 0.3: near the singularity it trades accuracy for sanity. The tip cannot reach the target anyway, but the joints stay below 1 rad/s and the arm points as close as it can. The project’s pinv_damped does the same.

Redundancy and the null space

What you are looking at

The same tracking task, position only, with three joints for two coordinates. Left: the arm and its earlier postures. Top right: the tip error. Bottom right: the joint angles. Below: how far the joints are from the middle of their ranges, and the manipulability.

With one joint more than the task needs, infinitely many postures put the tip in the same place: the elbow can move while the tip stays still. Those motions form the null space of J: joint speeds that produce zero tip velocity. The CLIK law can add any motion projected into that space without touching the tip, and use it for a second goal: keep the joints away from their limits, stay far from singularities, avoid an obstacle. The Jaco2, with seven joints for a six-dimensional task, has exactly one such spare motion: the elbow swinging round.

$$\dot{\mathbf q} = J^\dagger(\dot{\mathbf x}_d + K\mathbf e) + \big(I - J^\dagger J\big)\,k_0\,\nabla_{\mathbf q} w(\mathbf q), \qquad J\big(I - J^\dagger J\big) = 0$$
  1. I − J⁺J projects any joint motion onto the null space.
  2. Choose w(q) to maximise: −½|q − q_mid|² (centre the joints) or √det(JJᵀ) (manipulability).
  3. Its gradient, times k₀, is the joint motion you would like; the projection keeps only the harmless part.

Worked example

Centring the joints (ranges centred on 0°, 90°, 90°): the distance from the centres falls from 0.87 to 0.59 rad during the square, the arm ending at (−29°, 74°, 93°) instead of (−40°, 90°, 61°), while the tip error stays at 0.1 mm.

Common mistake

Adding the second goal’s gradient directly to q̇. Without the projection it moves the tip; with it, the tip does not notice.

Try it

Choose “Stay still”: the tip stays put while the arm folds itself into a new posture.

No second goal

J⁺ alone gives the smallest joint speeds at every instant; the posture wanders with the path and ends almost where it started (0.86 rad from the centres).

Manipulability

The spare motion now raises √det(JJᵀ) from 1.03 to 1.07, bending the elbow more (105°) to keep the tip far from singular postures. The joints end further from their centres: the two goals differ.

Self-motion

The tip is told to stay still; only the null-space term acts. The arm reconfigures to (−29°, 74°, 93°) and the tip moves by less than 0.03 mm. This is how a redundant robot reaches round an obstacle without moving its hand.

About the model

Kinematic simulation, as in the project’s tasks 1–3: joint velocities integrated exactly with Euler steps (Ts = 10 ms), no dynamics. Links 1, 0.7, 0.4 m. Square: 40 cm, 3 s per side with cruise speed 1.5 L/T, 0.5 s at the corners and 2 s at the end (15.5 s). Manipulability gradient by central differences. Tests: path duration and corners; J⁺ keeps the error below 1 mm and Jᵀ lags more; the angle is held in the pose task; the null-space term moves the joints towards their centres (or raises manipulability) while the tip error stays below 1 mm; near the singularity J⁺ needs over 20 times the joint speed of damped least squares.
