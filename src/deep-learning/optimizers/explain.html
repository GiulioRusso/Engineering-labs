What you are looking at

A loss surface over two parameters: height = loss (log-compressed so walls and floor both show), contour lines drawn on the surface, the red cross = the common starting point. Four optimizers walk down from it: gradient descent (grey), momentum (blue), RMSProp (green), Adam (orange). Bottom: each one's loss against the step. Drag to rotate.
$$\text{GD: } w\leftarrow w-\eta g\qquad \text{Momentum: } v\leftarrow\beta v+g,\ w\leftarrow w-\eta v\qquad \text{RMSProp: } s\leftarrow0.9s+0.1g^2,\ w\leftarrow w-\eta\,\frac{g}{\sqrt s}\qquad \text{Adam: momentum}\ \div\ \sqrt{s}$$
  1. GD steps straight down the steepest slope: fine on round bowls, slow and bouncy in long narrow valleys.
  2. Momentum keeps a running sum of past gradients: the bounces cancel, the consistent direction builds up speed.
  3. RMSProp divides each direction by its typical gradient size: steep directions get small steps, flat ones big steps.
  4. Adam does both.

Common mistake

Comparing optimizers at one learning rate. Each has its own good range: the fairest picture is each at its best η.

A narrow ravine

Curvature 12 across, 1 along. GD's step must be small for the steep direction, so it crawls along the gentle one. Rule: GD diverges if η > 2/λ_max = 2/12 ≈ 0.17.

η = 0.15

Close to the limit: GD jumps from wall to wall; momentum is also oscillating but moves along the valley.

η = 0.18

Above 2/12: each bounce is larger than the last — GD diverges. RMSProp and Adam, which rescale the steep direction, stay stable.

The banana valley

Rosenbrock's function: finding the valley is easy, following its curved floor to the minimum at (1, 1) takes most of the time.

Adam alone

Normalised steps of size ≈ η: it follows the floor at a steady pace.

A saddle point

Starting almost exactly on the ridge, the gradient across is tiny. GD needs many steps for the small offset to grow; momentum and Adam leave sooner. In high-dimensional networks saddles are far more common than bad local minima.

Noisy gradients

Mini-batch noise knocks the point off the ridge immediately: noise can help.

Two valleys

A shallow valley near the start and a deeper one further away. Which one an optimizer ends in depends on its path.

β = 0.95

Built-up speed carries momentum over the ridge between the valleys.

Noise

Noisy steps explore: each optimizer may end in a different valley than without noise.
